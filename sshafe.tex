\begin{abstract}
Remote server administration over SSH remains one of the most routine operations across cloud platforms, enterprise infrastructure, and embedded systems. However, SSH has significant shortcomings when it comes to detecting attacks and credential rotation for compromised user accounts. This study focuses on covering both, delivering a real-time attack detection system and novel cryptographic password rotation flow. A real-time attack detection system based on time series analysis of the authentication logs followed by locking out accounts under attack ensures a fail-closed system whereas a novel cryptographic password reset flow involving ECDHE in WebAuthn mitigates attacks involving sessions during password rotation.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
While SSH supports both public-key and password-based authentication, passwords continue to dominate in practice because they allow users to connect from any machine, not just those that carry a private key. This convenience comes at a cost. Passwords are a natural target for automated attacks where adversarial tools cycle through vast credential lists at high speed until one combination succeeds. The standard defensive response, blocking an IP address after a threshold number of failed login attempts, is fundamentally flawed. Attackers have learned to slow down or spread their activity across multiple IP addresses, staying just under whatever detection limit is in place. This leaves a significant, well-documented gap between what rule-based tools like Fail2Ban can detect and what real-world attackers do.

The complexity of this problem lies in post-detection. Most systems respond by locking the targeted account and directing the user through a recovery flow that relies on email links, one-time passwords sent by SMS, or similar out-of-band verification mechanisms. These approaches introduce a fresh set of vulnerabilities: Email-based recovery links can be phished, OTPs be intercepted, etc. The session tokens used to maintain the binding between the person who authenticated and the person entering the new password are frequently misconfigured, long-lived, or exposed to CSRF attacks. Detection and recovery are two halves of a problem that existing solutions address poorly in isolation and rarely in combination which this study aims to solve.

\section{Conclusion}

This study shows that the proposed lightweight, reversible perturbation can secure IIoT telemetry without compromising data utility for authorized receivers, evaluated against both static and adaptive adversaries. All four tested methods achieved 100\% reversibility and close to 50\% adversarial accuracy. XOR 64-bit masking proved to be the optimal solution across privacy, security, and implementation simplicity, primarily through its SIMD-enabled speed rather than an expanded keyspace. Further research should investigate cryptographically secure keystream generation (e.g., ChaCha20 or ASCON-128a in place of the prototype’s non-cryptographic PRNG) alongside key/seed rotation to achieve forward secrecy, streaming perturbation for telemetry pipeline processing, and performance benchmarking under real-world conditions on physical IIoT boards.
